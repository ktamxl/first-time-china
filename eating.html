Chapter 05 · Eating

Eating well, without reading Chinese

The best restaurant in a Chinese city is rarely the one with an English menu. It is the one near the top of a local ranking, with a queue of people who live there.

Checked November 2026

The Dianping routine

Dianping 大众点评 is the restaurant app Chinese diners actually use — part review site, part booking system, part discount marketplace. It has no English interface, and you do not need one if you follow the structure rather than the words.

  1. Set the city. Top-left, the city name. Do this first or you will be reading about the wrong place.
  2. Open the food tab. The second tab along the bottom. Within it, look for the ranking entries — 热门榜 (trending), 必吃榜 (must-eat) or 好评榜 (top rated). These are curated by the platform, not by advertisers, and they are a good shortlist.
  3. Open a restaurant and find the dishes. Inside the listing, the 推荐菜 section lists the dishes other diners ordered most, usually with a photo and a count. Order from that list and you will eat what the kitchen is good at.
  4. Look for a coupon. 代金券 is a prepaid voucher — pay ¥90 for ¥100 of food, or a set menu at a fixed price. Buy it in the app and show the code when you pay. This is where a family meal gets noticeably cheaper.
  5. Join the queue remotely. 排队 takes a number in the app before you arrive. On a weekend evening this can save an hour of standing outside.
Translation, quickly

Screenshot the listing and translate the image, or copy the Chinese name into a note. Show the name — not the English — to the taxi driver.

Reservations

Most mid-range restaurants do not take bookings at all: you turn up and queue. For the places worth planning around, there are four routes, in order of reliability:

RouteHowNotes
Hotel conciergeAsk the front desk, in person or by WeChat, a day or two ahead.Best for Michelin and Black Pearl rooms, and for a table of eight. Hotels have relationships; you do not.
The restaurant's WeChat accountSearch the restaurant name in WeChat, follow the official account, use the booking menu.Works for groups with a booking system. Chinese-only, but the menu structure is obvious.
Dianping queueTake a number remotely, then arrive close to your slot.Not a true reservation — miss your number and you start again.
PhoneAsk the hotel to call on your behalf.Only worth attempting with a Chinese speaker.

Expect a deposit for popular places at peak times, especially weekends and public holidays. The concierge will usually handle it against your room or ask for a card.

Private rooms 包间

Most Chinese restaurants of any size have private rooms upstairs. They are not only for banquets: a family of four with small children can book one, and it changes the meal.

  • Why it helps: children can move around without disturbing a room; the noise level drops; you can leave the table without losing it; and the service is more attentive.
  • What it costs: usually a minimum spend rather than a room fee — a few hundred yuan per person at mid-range places, more at the top end. For a family that was going to order properly anyway, the difference is small.
  • How to ask: “有包间吗？” — do you have a private room? Show it written down, or ask the concierge to arrange it.
Worth it with children

A private room turns a two-hour dinner from a negotiation into a relaxed evening. On a trip where every meal is in a loud room, book one or two and notice the difference.

Michelin and Black Pearl

Two rankings are worth knowing, and they answer different questions.

MICHELIN Guide — international, stars and Bib Gourmand, published for Shanghai, Guangzhou, Beijing, Chengdu and Hong Kong/Macau. Use the guide's own site or app; the starred list is short and reliable, and the Bib Gourmand list is better value.

Black Pearl 黑珍珠 — China's own restaurant ranking, run through Meituan and Dianping, graded one to three diamonds. It is the list Chinese diners actually argue about, and it covers cities and cuisines Michelin does not. You will find it inside Dianping or Meituan under the ranking entries.

Which to trust

Michelin for technique and service; Black Pearl for what a well-off local family would book. They overlap less than you would think, and the overlap is where the best meals are.

Ordering, once you are seated

  • QR menus are normal. Scan the code on the table, order and pay in the app. If it defeats you, ask for a paper menu — most places have one.
  • Dishes arrive when they are ready, not in courses. Rice comes separately; ask for it at the start if children are hungry.
  • Tea is usually charged per person, and some restaurants add a service fee. Both are normal.
  • Say “not spicy” twice. 不要辣 — bú yào là. In some kitchens “a little spicy” is still too much for a child.
  • Order less than you think. Portions are generous and sharing is the norm; adding a dish later is easy.

Delivery to your hotel room

Meituan 美团 and its delivery arm 美团外卖 will bring restaurant food, groceries, pharmacy items and travel essentials to the hotel door, usually within 30–45 minutes and often for less than a Western delivery fee.

  1. Set the address to the hotel, not the room. Pin the building on the map, then put the hotel name and your room number in the notes field. Riders navigate to pins, not to addresses.
  2. Choose “meet in the lobby”. Many hotels will not let a rider upstairs, and the rider will call — a call you cannot understand. Meet them at reception instead.
  3. Order earlier than you are hungry. 11:30 and 17:30 are the rush; a 12:15 order can arrive at 13:15.
  4. Pay in the app. Card or wallet, both work; no cash needed at the door.
  5. For medicine, use the pharmacy section 买药 — it delivers over-the-counter items, and the listing states which pharmacy is sending.
What this solves

A jet-lagged child, a rainy evening, a grandparent who does not want another restaurant: delivery turns a bad hour into a normal one. It is also how you buy a phone charger, nappies or bottled water at 11pm.
