When something goes wrong

China is safe for travellers. The problems that actually happen are administrative: a lost passport, a dead phone, a payment that will not clear. Here is the order to solve them in.

Save these before you need them, and save your hotel's front desk number too — in practice, the hotel is the fastest route to every one of the above, because they can explain the address and the situation in Chinese.

Hospitals and pharmacies

For anything serious, go to an international clinic in Shanghai or Shenzhen, or a private hospital in Hong Kong. They have English-speaking staff, take international insurance, and are used to visitors. Public hospitals are cheaper and excellent, but registration is in Chinese and the queue is long.

  • Bring your passport and your insurance details — payment is usually expected at the time, then claimed back.
  • Keep every receipt and diagnosis, in English if you can get it.
  • Pharmacies 药店 are on most streets. Show symptoms through translation, or bring the box of what you normally take.
  • Delivery works for medicine too. Meituan will bring over-the-counter medicine and travel essentials to a hotel — see delivery.

Lost passport

  1. Report it to the police. You need the report — every later step asks for it. The hotel can help you file it.
  2. Contact your embassy or consulate. Bring photographs, the police report and any copy of the passport you have. Save the address and number before you fly.
  3. Get an exit document. A replacement passport is not enough to leave; the local exit-entry administration must issue an exit permit, which usually takes a few working days.
  4. Tell your airline once you have the new document, so the booking can be updated.

Consular districts matter: in China, the consulate that serves you depends on the province you are in, not the nearest big city. Check which one covers Shanghai, Guangdong or Hong Kong before you need it.

Lost or dead phone

  • Freeze the wallets first. Alipay and WeChat Pay can be frozen from another device or by a companion — do this before anything else.
  • Block the SIM with your carrier, and change the password on your main email.
  • Use Find My or Find My Device if it is only misplaced; hotels recover phones constantly.
  • Have a paper backup — hotel address, a companion's number, and your passport copy. A dead phone should not leave you with no information at all.

When a payment will not clear

Work down this ladder rather than arguing with the app:

  1. Try the other wallet. Alipay failing and WeChat working — or the reverse — is the single most common fix, especially with taxi drivers receiving into personal accounts.
  2. Turn off any VPN and try again. A proxy can trip the risk checks.
  3. Ask to pay the merchant's business QR rather than a personal one.
  4. Use cash. It is legal tender and accepted; carrying ¥300–500 solves most dead-ends.
  5. Use a physical card at hotels and larger shops.

The scams that still exist

  • The tea-house invitation. A friendly stranger suggests a traditional tea ceremony; the bill arrives in the thousands. Decline invitations from people who approach you.
  • Unauthorised taxis. Anyone offering a car inside a terminal is not a licensed taxi. Use the official queue or DiDi.
  • The “art student” exhibition. A polite stranger invites you to view student work; it ends in high-pressure sales.
  • Fake police. Real officers do not ask for your bank card or a payment to resolve an issue on the street.

None of this is common, and none of it should make you anxious. The pattern in every case is the same: someone approaches you first, in English, with an opportunity. A polite “no” ends it.
