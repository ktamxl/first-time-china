Chapter 07 · Extras

Small pleasures

Three things that cost little, take an hour, and become the photographs and stories you actually bring home.

Checked November 2026

Hire the photographer standing there

At most major sights in China you will see photographers with a camera and a small display of prints. They are not a scam and they are not expensive: typically ¥100–300 for a set of edited photographs, delivered by WeChat or AirDrop within the hour.

The value is not the camera. It is that they know exactly where to stand, at what time, and how to pose you — including the poses that make a family look relaxed rather than lined up against a wall. You will get pictures nobody in your group could have taken of all of you together.

  • Agree first: price, number of photographs, edited or not, and how they will be delivered. Show the numbers on your phone.
  • Ask to see recent work on their phone before you start.
  • Pay a deposit or the whole amount after — never before you see the files.
  • Good locations: the Bund at dusk, Yu Garden, the West Lake causeways, Victoria Peak, the Shenzhen bayfront.
Best hour of the trip

Book the last hour of daylight. The light is kind, the crowds thin, and you end the day with photographs instead of another restaurant receipt.

Hanfu and cosplay photo sessions

Period-costume photography is a mainstream Chinese pastime, not a niche hobby. Studios rent hanfu 汉服 or Republican-era dress, do hair and make-up, and shoot you in a historic quarter for an hour or two. Packages typically run ¥200–600 including costume, styling and a set of retouched images.

  • Where: historic districts and water towns — Yuyuan and Zhujiajiao in Shanghai, the old streets of Suzhou and Hangzhou, and photo studios around them.
  • Booking: walk in, or find them on Dianping under photography 写真; hotel concierges know the good ones.
  • Time: allow two to three hours, most of it hair and make-up.
  • Children love it, and a family group in matching costume is a photograph you will keep.

Cosplay of a different kind — anime, games, film characters — clusters around comic conventions and specific shopping districts. If that is the interest, ask the hotel where the nearest 漫展 or costume studio is, or search Xiaohongshu for the city plus the character.

Bars and clubs

Nightlife is concentrated, late and largely found through Chinese apps rather than English websites.

CityWhere to startCharacter
ShanghaiJing'an, around Found 158 and the streets off Nanjing West Road; the Bund for cocktail bars with a viewCocktail culture, rooftop bars, clubs that fill after 11pm
ShenzhenCoco Park in Futian; Sea World in ShekouYounger, louder, more casual; expat-friendly in Shekou
Hong KongLan Kwai Fong and SoHo, Central; Knutsford Terrace in Tsim Sha TsuiLate, international, easy to navigate in English

How to find something specific

  • Dianping — the nightlife category 酒吧 or 夜店, sorted by rating; photographs and reviews tell you the dress code faster than any English list.
  • Xiaohongshu 小红书 — where young Chinese plan nights out. Search the city plus “bar” and translate the results.
  • The concierge — for guest lists, tables and whether a place is still good this month. Clubs change names and owners quickly.
  • Meituan — for table packages and drink deals, often cheaper than the door.

Practical notes

  • Bring your passport. Clubs and some bars check ID, and a foreign licence is not always accepted.
  • Watch your drink, as you would anywhere, and stay with your group.
  • Agree the taxi or DiDi home before midnight — surge and availability both get worse after 2am.
  • KTV KTV is the other national night out: a private room, a screen, food and singing. Book a room for the family early evening; it is sober, fun and works with children.
