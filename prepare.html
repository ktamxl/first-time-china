Before you fly

Everything on this page is cheaper and calmer to solve at home than at an airport counter. Work through it a month out, not the night before.

Checked November 2026

The checklist

  • ☐ Passport valid for at least six months, with blank pages
  • ☐ The right to enter: a visa, a visa-free scheme, or transit — checked against the official list
  • ☐ Return or onward ticket, and the first hotel booking, both printable
  • ☐ Travel insurance that explicitly covers mainland China
  • ☐ Both payment apps installed and verified with your passport
  • ☐ eSIM bought or roaming enabled, and tested
  • ☐ Trip.com account with every traveller added, passports and all
  • ☐ Prescriptions in original packaging, with a doctor's letter
  • ☐ Home phone number kept active for bank verification codes
  • ☐ Copies of every passport page — paper and cloud
  • ☐ Hotel name and address saved in Chinese characters
  • ☐ Your embassy or consulate's address and number saved offline

Getting in: three routes

RouteWho it suitsWhat to know
Tourist visa (L)Anyone staying longer, or whose nationality is not on a visa-free listApply at a Chinese visa centre weeks ahead. Flights and hotels are usually required with the application.
Visa-free entryPassport holders from countries with a unilateral visa-free arrangementTime-limited and often for tourism or business only. The country list changes — verify before booking.
240-hour visa-free transitPassport holders from the eligible countries, transiting to a third countryYou must arrive from one country and depart to a different one, and stay inside the permitted region. Currently available to 57 nationalities.
Check this twice, and once more

The transit list changed as recently as August 2026, when Kyrgyzstan and Vietnam were added. Rules that were true for a friend last year may not apply to you. Confirm on the National Immigration Administration site or your embassy's page before you pay for flights.

Two details that catch people: the passport must usually be valid for six months beyond the trip, and transit routes require a genuinely onward ticket to a third country — a return to where you came from does not qualify.

Medication

Bring prescriptions in their original packaging, with a letter from your doctor naming the drug, the dose and the reason. Keep them in hand luggage, not checked bags.

Some common Western drugs are prohibited

Amphetamine-based medication for ADHD is the best-known example; several other controlled drugs are restricted or banned outright. Arriving with them is a serious legal matter, not a paperwork problem. If you take anything controlled, check with the Chinese embassy in your country before you fly — and if in doubt, leave it at home and travel with a substitute your doctor approves.

Bank codes and the two-factor trap

Your bank will send a verification code by SMS at the worst possible moment — usually when you are trying to unfreeze a card. If you replace your home SIM with a Chinese one, that code goes to a number you can no longer receive.

  • Keep your home number alive on an eSIM or a second slot, with roaming for incoming SMS only.
  • Tell the bank you are travelling, and check that your card works abroad before you leave.
  • Install the bank's app and log in at home — some banks require a trusted device before they will show anything.
  • Carry a second card from a different issuer, kept separately.

Insurance

Check the policy wording for the word “China” specifically. Some travel policies exclude the mainland, or cover it only for medical evacuation and not treatment. Save the assistance number as a contact and a screenshot — not only in an email you cannot open offline.

The holiday calendar

Chinese public holidays move the entire country at once, and transport sells out. This is the single most useful planning fact in the guide.

PeriodWhenWhat it does to travel
Spring FestivalLate January to mid-FebruaryThe largest human migration on earth. Trains and flights sell out weeks ahead; cities empty and restaurants close. Avoid.
QingmingEarly AprilA long weekend of grave-visiting. Regional trains busy.
Labour Day1–5 MayA week of domestic travel. Attractions at capacity.
Dragon BoatJuneShort break; local festivals, busy riverside cities.
Mid-AutumnSeptember or early OctoberShort break; can merge with National Day.
National Day1–7 OctoberThe week to avoid. Hundreds of millions travel; trains, sights and hotels at their worst.
Why avoid October, specifically

It is not only crowds. For many workers this is the one week a year they can get home to their parents and children. A seat you take is a seat someone else needed more. Going in a different week is better for you and kinder to them.

Arrival admin nobody mentions

  • Hotel registration. Every foreign guest must be registered, and a minority of hotels cannot or will not take foreign passports. Confirm before booking, and bring the passport to check-in for every guest, including children.
  • Security screening. Bags are scanned entering metros, railway stations, malls and many museums. It is quick, but it adds ten minutes everywhere — build it into timings.
  • Addresses run backwards. City, district, road, building, floor, room. When talking to a driver, a landmark beats a street number: “opposite the mall” works, a postcode does not.
  • Tax refund. Foreign visitors can reclaim roughly 11% VAT on purchases above a minimum spend per store, claimed at the airport before departure with the passport and receipts. Most visitors never bother; on a large purchase it is worth the queue.
